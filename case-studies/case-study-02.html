Introduction

I took Interaction Design from Dan Hatch, where we spent the semester walking through a full redesign process for the UVU CET website—iterating alongside the coursework and applying each lesson to the same project.

What happened

We moved through each phase of ideation and execution in Figma (no coding). First came sketches exploring layout ideas for CET. Then low-fidelity wireframes, balancing UVU branding with fresh structure per Dan’s guidelines. High-fidelity wireframes followed, refining the look and flow before prototyping.

In prototyping, we linked pages and interactions to behave like a real site. We then ran eye-tracking user tests with friends and family, assigning tasks and measuring where attention landed. Insights from testing drove another round of refinements and a second test loop before final presentation at semester’s end.

Conclusion

This class showed how iterative UX work balances function and design while responding to user needs. Repeated testing clarified how small changes improved the experience, and reinforced the importance of staying true to brand while modernizing the interface.
